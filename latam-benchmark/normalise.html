How normalisation works

Money basis: the Benchmark page shows nominal USD as reported; this page restates every project in 2024 USD (US PPI Final Demand plus a technology cost trend), on AC capacity and plant-only scope. Confidence labels (reported / derived / estimate) are explained on the Benchmark page.

  • fyear – inflation (US PPI) and technology cost trend to 2024 USD
  • fAC/DC – converts solar MWp (DC) to grid MW (AC)
  • fscope – strips grid, land, development, BESS; grosses up loans and turbine-only figures
  • fsize – optional economies of scale (off by default)
  • fsite – remote desert / high-altitude uplift
  • fFX – which BRL/USD year is used
Full explanation of each factor

Reported cost per MW is divided by a product of six correction factors. Each factor is 1.0 when the project already matches the base case (2024 US dollars, AC grid capacity, plant-only scope, 200 MW, no battery). A factor above 1 means the reported number is inflated relative to the base (so normalising lowers it); below 1 means it understates (normalising raises it). Change any default in the left panel and every table and chart updates instantly.

  • fyear – when the money was spent. Corrects for inflation and for technology getting cheaper. Inflation uses the US PPI Final Demand index (BLS series WPSFD4; annual averages via FRED PPIFIS). Trend defaults: solar −5 %/yr, wind −2 %/yr, judgement anchored on IRENA Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2023/2024 (installed-cost declines). Matters most for solar, where module prices roughly halved 2022–24. Change: “cost trend %/yr”.
  • fAC/DC – what “MW” means. Many solar projects quote panel (DC peak) capacity, which is ~25 % larger than grid (AC) capacity, so cost per MWp looks cheap. Default 1.25, typical utility-scale ILR (NREL/LBNL Utility-Scale Solar range 1.2–1.35). Where the source gives both DC and AC, the exact ratio is used. Change: “DC/AC ratio”.
  • fscope – what the number covers. A full budget includes development, grid and land (default ×1.10 vs plant-only); EPC ≈ plant (1.00); turbine supply alone is ~70 % of onshore wind capex (IRENA: turbines 64–84 %); a loan covers ~70 % of capex (typical LatAm project-finance leverage), so loan amounts are grossed up by 1/0.70. Battery-included rows are divided by ×1.35 (rough; actual depends on MWh). Classified from each row’s “amount type” and notes. Change: the five scope inputs.
  • fsize – economies of scale. (MW/200)b. Default b = 0 (off) because the dataset shows no clear scale effect. Try b = −0.1 to test a mild scale benefit.
  • fsite – hard sites. Uplift only where the source places the project in a remote desert (×1.05) or above 2,000 m (×1.08); flags in the site_flag column, set for 6 rows only. Defaults are author judgement – edit freely.
  • fFX – which exchange rate. BRL rows were converted at the IRS yearly-average rate of the stated year. “Signing-year” uses the year before COD/inauguration for built projects; “build-year” uses the year after a filing/financing date. fFX = rate used ÷ alternative rate. USD rows = 1.0.

Limits. This cannot fix wrong or incomplete source figures, unknown battery sizes, hidden transmission lines, local-content premia, or pre-FID filing values that never get built. Scope factors are averages, not project-specific. Cost per MW ignores resource quality (Chilean sites produce far more MWh per MW). Normalised numbers are comparisons, not quotes.

Methods note

Methods note

  • Input: data_v2.csv (76 rows, public sources). Offshore wind excluded. Site flags added in data_norm.csv.
  • Year = the row’s stated year (filing, FID or COD — mixed). 2026 rows use 2025 PPI (latest full year).
  • Scope classes: loan = amount type mentions debt/financing/loan; EPC = EPC or construction capex; turbine = supply-only; otherwise full. BESS = technology includes BESS or notes say “includes BESS/storage”.
  • IRS yearly average BRL/USD: 2020 5.151, 2021 5.395, 2022 5.165, 2023 4.994, 2024 5.392, 2025 5.593.
  • Medians use filtered rows; hybrids are grouped as their own technology.
